Comments

Comments sit beside a note, never inside its text: the note's Markdown, its versions, its tasks and its tags stay as they are. They sync with the vault to your other devices, like the note.

Commenting

  • On text: select words in the editor or in the preview. In the editor, Comment is a button of the selection toolbar that shows above the selection, after the formatting tools. In the preview, the same toolbar shows with Comment and Copy (on a touch screen, below the selection, clear of the system's own selection menu). Choose Comment, or press Ctrl+Shift+M (Cmd+Shift+M on a Mac); you can change the key in Settings ▸ Keyboard. Alt+F10 moves into the toolbar and Escape leaves it. With the selection toolbar turned off in Settings ▸ Editor, a small Comment button shows above the selection in the editor instead.
  • On a section: hover a heading in the preview and choose the + in its margin, or press Ctrl+Shift+M with the cursor in the section and nothing selected in the editor.
  • On the whole note: Comment on note in the Comments panel, or in the note's menu (⋯).

The composer opens in the Comments panel. It is a small Markdown editor: bold, italics and links with the editor's keys, [[ to link a note, : for emoji. Pick a type (Note, Question, Suggestion, Issue, Review or To-do: the last one you used is chosen next time), check it with Preview, then Post (Ctrl+Enter). Cancel (Escape) keeps your draft on this device until you post it. Comments show Markdown, tables, code, emoji and note links; - [ ] is a plain list in a comment, never a task, and images, diagrams and math are not shown in 0.5.3.

The first time you post in a vault, Arkenda asks for your name in this vault, filled in with this device's name. It is shown on every comment you write in the vault; comments keep the name they were written with.

The Comments panel

Comments live in a panel of the note's inspector (the right sidebar, beside Outline and Backlinks; Ctrl+Alt+B shows or hides it). Its tabs are Open, Resolved and Orphaned, each with its count. Open threads are listed in note order, the whole-note ones first. Each thread shows the text it is about, its comments in order with their author and time, and what you can do: Reply, Edit and Delete on your own comments (those written on any of this vault's devices), Resolve, and ⋯ for Re-attach to selection and Copy link. Deleting the first comment deletes the whole thread; a deleted reply shows "Comment deleted".

The text of an open thread is tinted and underlined in the preview, with a marker in the margin that counts the threads of that paragraph; where threads overlap the tint is deeper. The editor shows a quiet underline and a mark in the gutter. Click a highlight, a marker or a gutter mark to select its thread in the panel.

Moving between threads

  • Alt+↓ and Alt+↑ go to the next and the previous thread of the selected tab, in note order (after the last comes the first again), from the preview or the Comments panel: the thread's card takes the focus and its text scrolls into view. Screen readers hear "Thread 2 of 5".
  • In the editor Alt+↑/↓ move lines, so there F8 and Shift+F8 do the same; they work everywhere else too. From the editor the cursor goes to the thread's line and the focus stays in the text.
  • On a focused card, Enter selects the thread at its text and R replies; ← and → switch the tabs. Escape closes a composer (the draft stays) and gives the focus back to where it was, or to the thread's card.

All of these keys can be changed in Settings ▸ Keyboard.

Links to a thread

⋯ ▸ Copy link on a thread copies a link such as /notes/<note>#comment-<thread>. Opening it (pasted into another note, or in the address bar of the web app) opens the note, shows the Comments panel on that thread with its card focused, and scrolls its text into view. When the thread was deleted, the note still opens and a short notice says the thread is no longer there.

On a phone or a tablet

On a phone the Comments panel is a bottom sheet (the note's Details): it opens at half the screen and grows with its threads; swipe it down, tap outside it or press back to close it. The composer is as wide as the sheet, with large targets; Escape on a keyboard closes the composer first and the sheet next. Select text and choose the Comment button below the selection, or Comment on note in the note's menu. On a tablet the panel opens as the inspector's drawer over the note.

Resolving

Resolve a thread once it is settled: its highlight goes and it moves to the Resolved tab, which shows who resolved it and when (the text gets a faint underline while that tab is open). Reopen brings it back, and so does a reply. Delete resolved threads… in the Resolved tab removes them from the note for good.

On a whiteboard

On a board (the Whiteboard experiment), right-click an element and choose Comment, or select one element and press Ctrl+Shift+M (Cmd+Shift+M on a Mac); with nothing selected the comment is on the whole board. The thread remembers the element's text, shown on its card as Board element.

An element with open threads carries a small pin at its top-right corner with their count; the pin turns grey when all of them are resolved. Click the pin to see that element's threads alone in the Comments panel (On this board element); × shows every thread of the board again.

Comments never enter the board's text. When you delete an element, its threads move to the Orphaned tab with the element's text, so nothing is lost: undo the delete and they come back, or select another element and choose Re-attach to selection.

On images, recordings and files

In the media library, open a file's details and choose Comments: a drawer shows its threads, and Comment starts one about the whole file. They work the same for images, audio and video (and any other file). A file's comments belong to the file itself, so they show wherever it is embedded, in every note and board; the drawer says how many places that is. The library's grid shows how many open threads each file has.

The same drawer opens from the media viewer of a note's preview: click an image you added to the note (or open a recording or video in the viewer) and choose the Comments button in the viewer's top bar, which shows how many open threads the item has. The drawer follows the item as you step through the note's media; keys typed in it stay there. Images linked from the web have no comments.

A file's comments stay with the vault for as long as the vault has them: deleting the file from the media library's trash removes it from this device only, and another device may still hold it, so its comments are kept.

When the text changes

A thread follows its text as you edit. When the text changed a little, the thread stays with a dashed underline and a Text changed flag. When the text is gone, the thread moves to the Orphaned tab with its original quote: select new text in the note and choose Re-attach to selection, or Resolve it.

Comments written by a newer Arkenda are shown but cannot be changed on this device.

Finding comments in search

The full-text search (Ctrl+Shift+F, or the notes in the command palette) also looks in comments. A note found only through one of its comments is marked Comment, shows the comment's words, and opens with the Comments panel on that thread. Comments on a whiteboard's elements count as the board note's. Open threads come before resolved ones, and deleted comments are never found. Comments never become tags, tasks or note links, even when they hold #words, - [ ] or [[links]]. Comments on media files are not searched.

Replies to your comments

When someone on another device replies in a thread where you (on any of this vault's devices) wrote a comment, the activity island in the title bar shows Reply to your comment, with the replier's name and, when there are several, how many. Hover it to see the note's title (unless note titles are hidden in the island). Open goes to the thread, Dismiss takes the notice away, and it also goes once you open the note. The notice never shows what the comment says. It comes for notes whose comments are open in Arkenda at that moment (a note open in a tab or a pane), and not for your own replies from your other devices.

In a shared vault

When you share a note or a notebook (the note's or the notebook's Share…), They can sets what the other person does with it:

  • Read only: they read the notes and their comments, with no composer.
  • Comment: they read the notes and comment on them: start threads, reply, edit and delete their own comments, resolve, reopen and re-attach threads. They cannot change the note's text, title or details.
  • Edit: they change the notes and comment on them, as a commenter does.

You, as the owner, can edit and delete any comment in what you shared. Your devices check every comment a member sends, so a member's device can only write what its role allows. A member still on Arkenda 0.5.2 who was given Comment sees the share as read only. Comments on media files stay in your vault: they do not travel in a share.

Exporting a note with its comments

In the note's Export… dialog, check Include comments (it is off by default) to add a Comments section after the note, as a web page, a PDF or plain text. It lists each open thread with the text or the target it is about, then its comments with their author, date and replies. Include resolved threads (available once comments are included) adds the resolved ones after them, with who resolved them. A deleted comment reads "Comment deleted", deleted threads are left out, and a thread whose text changed or is gone shows its original quote with a note saying so. The section is for reading in any browser or viewer; Arkenda does not import comments back from it.
