Media

Pictures, audio, video and other files live in your vault next to your notes. Everything here works on the device: nothing is sent to a service to be read, converted or recognised.

Adding files

  • Pick, drop or paste a file into a note (Attach a file… in the editor and on the whiteboard takes any kind, recognised from its contents): a picture shows in place, audio and video get a player, and any other file (a PDF, a zip, a spreadsheet…) becomes a card. An upload shows its progress and can be cancelled; a file that cannot be added says why.
  • Pasting a picture copied in another app (a screenshot, an image from a browser or a photo app) adds it to the note, on the desktop, the web and Android alike. On Android, Arkenda reads PNG, JPEG, WebP and GIF pictures of up to 16 MB from the clipboard.
  • Dropping files from your file manager works in the desktop app too, onto a note or a whiteboard. On Linux the app reads the dropped files itself first, so a large video takes a moment before its upload appears; a file larger than 2 GB is not added.
  • On a phone or tablet, the insert menu also has Take photo, Record video and Record audio, which open the device's camera or recorder.
  • A voice memo is recorded inside Arkenda, from quick capture's microphone button or Voice memo in the command palette (the web and the desktop app; see the activity island). It is stored like an upload (the library, the trash, sync and playback work the same) and added to your inbox note as a line that plays it. A WebM memo shows a video player for now.
  • A screenshot is taken from quick capture's camera button or Screenshot in the command palette (the desktop app on Linux and Windows; see the activity island), cropped to a region in Arkenda or kept whole, stored as a PNG like an upload, and added to your inbox note as a line that shows it; Screenshot into this note puts it at the cursor of the open note instead.
  • A screen recording (an experiment, off by default: Settings ▸ Experimental ▸ Screen recording) is recorded inside Arkenda from quick capture's screen button or Record screen in the command palette (the desktop app on Linux and Windows; see the activity island): VP8 WebM, at most 1920 px and 30 frames a second, stopped at 30 minutes or 492 MB so it still syncs. It is stored like an upload and added to your inbox note as a line that plays it.
  • Video formats: MP4, MOV, WebM and MKV (Matroska), found from the file's bytes, never its name. An MKV plays in the note where the app's web view can decode it (the Linux desktop app plays most; on Windows and Android it depends on the codecs inside); where it can't, the player says it can't be streamed here and offers Open in system player. Arkenda 0.1.0 and 0.1.1 don't know MKV: they show such an embed as a broken picture and don't fetch its file from your other devices (an S3 mirror copies it as a plain file) until they are updated. An MKV added before this version stays a .bin file card.
  • Adding the same file again inserts a new reference to the copy already stored; it is never stored twice.
  • Image compression on upload (Settings ▸ Editor): Balanced (the default: at most 2560 px on the long edge), High (1600 px, smaller files) or Off (the photo as it is).
  • Location is removed from photos on upload unless you turn off Remove location from photos on upload (Settings ▸ Editor, on each device). A compressed photo keeps no metadata at all.

Cards: Open and Save

A file card shows the name, type and size. Open hands the file to an app of your device (a PDF viewer, for example), and Save lets you choose where to put a copy. On the web, Save downloads it.

  • A PDF card also has Read (see below), and on a computer or tablet Open beside.
  • A .csv or .json card has Preview: a table or a tree, 100 rows at a time.
  • A .zip, .tar.gz, .tgz or .tar card has Contents: the files inside, each with Preview and Save. Nothing inside an archive is ever run.

Galleries, players and photos

  • Two or more pictures in one paragraph (or on adjacent lines) become a gallery: a grid on a computer or tablet, a swipeable strip on a phone. A picture opens the viewer, which steps through its gallery; pinch or use the zoom buttons to look closer, and Copy image (⧉) puts the picture on the clipboard to paste into another app (on a phone too, where there is no right-click menu).
  • Timestamps: write [1:23] (or [1:02:03]) in the text and it becomes a link that jumps the nearest player above it to that time. Each player has Copy timestamp.
  • Capture frame on a video player saves the current frame as a picture just below the video.
  • Photo details (in the viewer and the media library) show the date taken, the camera and, when the photo still has it, the location.

Annotating and redacting a picture

Annotate (a picture's menu, or ✎ in the viewer) draws arrows, highlights, pen strokes, text and redaction boxes over a picture. The drawing is kept in the note, the picture itself is not changed, and undo works as for any edit.

Redaction boxes hide, they do not erase. The stored picture still holds the pixels under a box. Exports and Print use a new picture with the drawing burnt in, and never the original. To get rid of the hidden part, use Save a redacted copy: it stores a copy with the boxes burnt in and points the note at it. Then delete the original in the media library.

Reading PDFs

Read opens a PDF in Arkenda's reader: zoom, a page field and Bookmarks (the PDF's own outline). On a computer or tablet, Open beside shows it next to the note. Pages are drawn only as you reach them, so a large PDF opens quickly. Password-protected PDFs are not supported yet.

Select text, then Copy link to highlight (or Insert link in the note, when beside a note): the link opens the reader at that page with the passage marked. Copy link to page links to the page. The reader lists the notes that link to the PDF and their highlights.

A table of contents

A line with only [toc] shows the note's headings as links, and follows every edit. [toc:2] lists only the first two levels. It is in the / menu as Table of contents. Exports to the web and Print keep it as a list; the Markdown export keeps [toc] as written.

The media library

The media library (Media in the desktop sidebar, under More on the phone, Open the media library in the palette, or Settings ▸ Data & storage ▸ Storage) lists every stored file with where it is used ("Used in 2 places"). A file's Used in N places menu in a note opens the library at that file, in its folder.

  • Folders. On the desktop the folder tree is on the left (Attachments is the top level, with every file); on a tablet or phone, Folders opens it as a sheet. Breadcrumbs show where you are, and a folder shows its own files with its folders as tiles above them; Subfolders too adds the files of the folders inside. New folder, and a folder's menu (⋯ next to the breadcrumbs, or right-click a folder): Open, New folder inside, Rename…, Move to…, Download as .zip and Remove folder…. See Media folders.
  • Grid or list. The list shows each file's folder, size, date and uses. The phone starts with the list.
  • Search looks in every folder. Filters: type, notebook (the files that notes of one notebook use), Unused (with its total and Select all), In the trash and size. Sort by newest, name, largest or Most used.
  • Select files with their checkboxes (on a phone, Select first) for the bulk bar: Move to…, Download (one file as itself, two or more as one .zip named after the folder) and Delete. On the desktop you can also drag files (or the ticked ones) onto a folder in the tree, a folder tile or a breadcrumb (on touch, Move to… only). Every move shows a notice with Undo.
  • A file's details (click it; a sheet on touch): where it is used (each note or whiteboard opens at the reference), its type, size and date, its folder (Change…), the notebook rule of its folder, whether it syncs (images up to 10 MB, other files up to 500 MB travel to your other devices; larger ones stay on this device), and the notebooks of the notes that use it. A file used by notes of several notebooks stays in one folder.
  • Delete is safe: an unused file goes to this device's trash, and a file still in use first lists the notes that use it, then lets you choose; its embeds become "removed media" placeholders, which Restore turns back.

Media folders

Folders sort your files in the media library. A folder is a label, not a place on disk: every file stays where it is (attachments/<hash>.<ext>), so moving a file to a folder changes no link and no note.

  • Folders nest. A folder can hold files and other folders.
  • Removing a folder never deletes a file. Its files and folders go up to the folder above it (or to the top).
  • A notebook can name a folder. Media added to a note in that notebook goes to that folder. A notebook inside another follows its parent's folder unless it names its own.

The notebook's media folder. Right-click a notebook (on a phone, open it from Notes and long-press it) and choose Media folder…; the menu shows the folder it uses now, or "(inherited)" when it follows a parent's. Pick a folder, make one with New folder, or choose No folder (Attachments) to clear it, then Save. The dialog says when the notebook follows a parent's folder. From then on, every file you add to a note there (pasted, dropped, picked, inserted from the editor's media menu, added from the notch's shelf, put on a board, or a screenshot or voice memo saved to your Inbox note) goes to that folder, unless the file is already in a folder: a file belongs to one folder, so adding the same file to another notebook's note leaves it where it is. The library shows the rule on the folder and in a file's details.

  • Files already there. When the notebook's notes already use files, the dialog offers once, unticked, to put the files only this notebook's notes use in the new folder (files that other notebooks' notes use never move). Leave it unticked and only new files follow the rule.
  • Moving a note. When you move a note to a notebook whose folder is a different one, Arkenda asks Move its media too? for the files only that note uses that are still in the old notebook's folder, and lists them. Move its media too puts them in the new folder; Leave it changes nothing. Either way the note's text is not touched. Moving a note to a notebook with no folder, or with the same one, asks nothing.

How folders sync. Folders, labels and notebook folders travel with the vault, like your statuses and snippets. In a browser vault they sync with the vault itself. In a folder vault they live in .arkenda/media-folders.json and, with sync on, reach your other devices too; a cloud mirror carries the file as well. Edits made on two devices at once merge: a rename on one and a move on the other both survive. When both change the same thing (two names for one folder, two folders for one file), one of them wins, the same on every device; no file is ever lost. If media-folders.json is damaged, Arkenda keeps a *.corrupt-<time> copy and, with sync on, rewrites it from your other devices' copy; if it goes missing (deleted, or not delivered yet by a sync tool), Arkenda writes it again from that copy instead of treating your folders as deleted.

The trash

More ▸ Trash groups what you deleted by kind: notes, whiteboards and media. Press and hold an item (or use its menu) for Restore, Open, Select and Delete forever. On a phone, Empty trash is in the ⋯ menu at the top, and with items selected Restore and Delete forever stay at the bottom of the screen.

  • The trash is per device. Each device shows only what was moved to the trash on it.
  • Files in a browser vault (phones, the web) are stored in one place that every browser vault on the device shares, so deleting a file forever cannot free its space while another vault might use it. It leaves the trash and no longer shows in the media library; if a note uses it again, it shows again.
  • Moving to the trash and restoring sync; deleting forever does not. Delete forever cleans up this device only: a note leaves this device's trash and stays deleted (and hidden) on every device, and a file's space is freed on this device once no note uses it.

Storage

  • Keep offline (the default) keeps every file on the device. With an S3 mirror on the desktop app, Load on demand (Settings ▸ Sync & devices ▸ Cloud mirror ▸ Media) leaves large audio and video in the mirror and streams them when you open them; pictures always stay.

Posters and lengths from the system

Before Arkenda reads a video itself, it asks the system what it already knows: on Linux the thumbnail your file manager made (~/.cache/thumbnails), on Windows the shell's thumbnail and the video's length and size, on Android the media retriever's. Long videos then show their poster without Arkenda seeking into the file. What the system does not know, Arkenda reads as before; it waits at most 5 seconds for the system's answer.

Linux

On Linux, the desktop app's web view can't play the app's own media links, so a folder vault's audio and video play from a small private web server inside the app instead. Any size plays and seeks, as on other systems.

  • It starts the first time a window plays media, never at startup, and stops when the app closes.
  • It listens on this computer only (127.0.0.1, a port the system picks), never on the network.
  • Each window gets its own secret address, which stops working when that window reloads or closes. Without it the server answers nothing, and it only serves the media of the vault open in that window, checked against the vault's records like every other read.
  • At most 32 connections at once; a player paused for more than two minutes reconnects when it resumes.

A browser vault's media, media sealed with Encrypt media on this device, and any file when that server can't start still play inside the page: up to 64 MiB of any type, larger files only as fragmented MP4 or WebM. Convert others, for example with ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -c copy -movflags frag_keyframe+empty_moov out.mp4.

When a file can't be streamed or decoded here, the message offers Open in system player for a folder vault's file on this device: the app hands the vault's own file to your system's default player, without copying it.
